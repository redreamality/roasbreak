Contribution Margin vs Gross Margin

Gross margin stops after product cost. Contribution margin keeps subtracting the order costs that determine how much is actually available for advertising.

Direct answer

Use contribution margin to set an acquisition ceiling.

Gross margin is generally revenue minus cost of goods sold. Contribution margin subtracts additional costs that move with an order, such as fulfillment, payment fees, marketplace commissions, discounts, refunds, and expected return losses.

CostGross marginContribution margin
Product cost / COGSIncludedIncluded
Pick, pack, shipping subsidyUsually excludedIncluded
Payment and marketplace feesUsually excludedIncluded
Discounts and expected refundsDepends on revenue basisIncluded explicitly

Example: the missing costs change the target

A $120 order with $48 of product cost has 60% gross margin and a 1.67x gross-margin break-even ROAS. If fulfillment is $9, fees are 3%, and returns and discounts consume 8%, contribution falls to $49.80, or 41.5%. The operating break-even ROAS becomes 2.41x.

The difference is the ad budget that never existed. Using 1.67x as the campaign floor would scale orders while losing contribution.

Where to get the inputs

Use net product revenue with tax excluded. Pull landed product cost from inventory or finance records, fulfillment from your 3PL or shipping ledger, payment fees from the processor, and refund/return loss from mature order history.
